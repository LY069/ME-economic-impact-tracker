# WS5 — Strategic and private reserves, Japan & China: how long is the runway?

**As-of: 2026-09-14.** Prepared per `docs/00_shared_brief.md` evidence standards. Search budget for
this workstream was exhausted mid-pass (shared session-wide WebSearch cap); several Sept-2026 hard
data points (JP crude imports/refinery runs since May, JP LPG reserve level, China gas
consumption rate) could not be located and are marked `NA`. This is flagged, not hidden — see
**Uncertainties and gaps**.

## Bottom line

- **Japan's crude runway is long in days-of-cover terms but the binding constraint has shifted to
  naphtha (already cutting output) and to LNG price, not volume.** Japan mobilized roughly
  **115-125mn bbl** of national+private+joint reserves March-May 2026 and, as of the last
  confirmed reading (15 June 2026), was still running its private stockpile obligation at the
  reduced 55-day floor (was 70 days). Under a **full-closure scenario matching the post-9/11
  pipeline-shutdown reality** (Hormuz transit ~3% of normal, all Saudi crude forced back through
  the strait), Japan's usable crude cushion (~247mn bbl, calc) buys an estimated **28-118 weeks**
  depending on how much of the gap gets re-substituted with non-Gulf crude — a wide range because
  no September import/refinery-run figure was located. **Naphtha is the real near-term stress
  point**: crackers were already cutting output by March-May on structurally thin (a-few-weeks)
  stocks, well before crude reserves would bind.
- **China's runway is materially longer than Japan's in absolute terms** (an estimated
  ~750mn bbl usable commercial cushion, before touching a further ~360mn bbl SPR), but China has
  already run the single largest national stockpile deployment episode on record: in May 2026
  refineries processed an estimated **13.5mb/d while importing only 6.36mb/d** — a **7.1mb/d gap**
  bridged entirely from stocks. At that draw rate the commercial cushion alone would exhaust in
  **~15 weeks (~late Dec 2026)**; at the more moderate ~1mb/d draw rate reported in June 2026 it
  would last **~2 years**. One analyst view flagged "depletion toward operational minimums" as the
  **base case for Q3 2026 — i.e., now** — a call this report cannot independently confirm or
  refute with the data available in this pass, and which is in tension with China's own partial
  easing of fuel-export curbs in July 2026 (see Uncertainties).
- **IEA's 400mn-bbl collective release (11 March 2026, the largest in the agency's history) is a
  one-off buffer, not a flow.** No running total of what remains of that specific release as of
  September 2026 was found.

## Narrative

### 1. Japan — reserve stocks by holder and product

Japan's oil security architecture has three legally distinct tiers, plus product-specific
buffers:

- **National (government) reserves**: pre-war (31 Dec 2025), 146 days on the domestic
  consumption-day basis used by METI/ANRE (a different, larger denominator than the IEA's
  net-import-cover basis — the brief's Jan-2025 baseline of 120 days national is the IEA figure;
  the two are **not directly comparable** and conflating them overstates or understates cover
  depending on direction — flagged per evidence standard #6). Japan released this reserve in two
  tranches: **~30 days (8.5mn kL) from 16 March 2026** — the largest single release in Japan's
  history, explicitly coordinated with the March IEA collective action — and **a further ~20 days
  from 1 May 2026**, distributed via JOGMEC from 10 national storage bases to four refiners
  (ENEOS, Idemitsu Kosan, Cosmo Oil, Taiyo Oil). Cumulative national release to date: **~50 days'
  worth**, i.e. roughly a third of the pre-war national reserve.
- **Private (mandatory) reserves**: refiners/distributors must legally hold a minimum number of
  days of sales; that minimum was **cut from 70 to 55 days on 16 March 2026** (a 15-day relief,
  freeing stock for use without breaching the law) and — per the latest confirmation found,
  15 June 2026 — **remains at 55 days**. Actual private holdings were 101 days pre-war and an
  estimated 81 days by end-April 2026 (a secondary-aggregator figure, not independently
  cross-checked against a METI primary release in this pass), implying roughly 26 days of
  headroom above the reduced legal floor as of that reading.
- **Joint reserves with producer countries**: ~13mn bbl (7 days) held jointly with Saudi Aramco
  (at Kiire and Okinawa), UAE and Kuwait, under agreements giving Japan priority purchase rights
  in an emergency. An estimated 5 of the 7 days were drawn by end-March 2026, leaving roughly
  2 days' worth.
- **Refined products**: no METI/PAJ weekly product-level (gasoline/gasoil/kerosene/jet/fuel-oil)
  barrel figures for mid-to-late 2026 were located in this pass (PAJ appears to have altered its
  weekly publication from around 21 March 2026; METI publishes with an ~2-month lag, so the
  latest available official reading at any point in 2026 trails "today" by roughly that much).
  This is a genuine gap, not an omission — flagged `NA` in the CSVs rather than estimated.
- **Naphtha**: the one product with clear, recent, hard evidence of stress. 60% of Japan's
  naphtha supply was imported (2024 baseline), 73.6% of that from the Middle East, so **>40% of
  total naphtha supply is Hormuz-exposed**. Japanese petrochemical crackers structurally hold only
  a few weeks of naphtha feedstock and were **already cutting output in March-May 2026** — before
  crude reserves came anywhere near a floor. Naphtha prices rose an estimated 60% on the back of
  the blockade. This is the clearest real-world demand-destruction signal in the whole dataset.
- **LPG**: Japan operates a dedicated national LPG stockpile system, but **no current 2026
  day-count or tonnage figure was found** in this search pass — marked `NA`. This is flagged as a
  priority follow-up, since the task brief specifically calls it out as material.
- **LNG**: **not stockpilable at scale**, and the data bears this out. Utility LNG tank stocks
  were ~2.3mn tonnes in early January 2026 (23% above Jan-2025, itself described as
  above-5-year-average) and still ~2.29mn tonnes by mid-April 2026 — essentially flat through the
  first two months of the war, because Japan's LNG portfolio is heavily diversified away from the
  Gulf (only ~6% from Qatar+UAE per the shared brief) even though Qatari cargoes physically
  transit Hormuz. JERA (Japan's largest power generator) described stocks as "sufficient" for
  August-October 2026 as of end-July, despite summer heat-driven draws. **The constraint is
  showing up as price, not volume**: the JKM spot benchmark was up 117.9% y/y to $24.69/MMBtu by
  9 September 2026, implying the global LNG market (in which Qatar is a top-two supplier and
  Hormuz LNG-tanker transits collapsed from 53.2/day pre-war to ~2/day) is tight enough that
  Japan is paying up for substitute cargoes rather than running short physically — so far. The
  commonly-cited **~2-3 weeks of utility tank cover** is the right order of magnitude for how
  quickly a genuine physical interruption (as opposed to a price shock) would bind, but this
  session could not re-verify a hard mid/late-2026 weeks-of-use figure.

### 2. China — reserve stocks by holder and product

China publishes no official SPR or commercial-inventory data; every figure here is a
**clearly-labelled third-party estimate** (Kpler, Vortexa, Energy Aspects, or aggregator analysis
built on the gap between customs import data and refinery throughput). Best current estimate
entering 2026 (Project 54, drawing on Kpler/Vortexa/EIA methodology): **~1.4bn bbl total crude
inventory**, split roughly **~360mn bbl government-held (SPR)** and **~1.0bn bbl commercial**,
equivalent to an estimated **~121 days of import cover** — a net-import basis, not consumption or
IEA-member basis, and well above the IEA's 90-day member benchmark (China is not an IEA member).

China's response to the war has been **inventory drawdown, not announced SPR release**: refiners
kept processing crude at close to normal (even elevated) rates while imports collapsed. The
starkest single data point: in **May 2026, refineries processed an estimated 13.5mb/d of crude
while imports were only 6.36mb/d — a 7.14mb/d gap financed entirely out of stocks**, described by
one source as the largest single-nation strategic-inventory deployment event on record. Q2-2026
customs data (hard, GACC) confirms the collapse at the aggregate level: imports averaged 8.1mb/d,
down 32% quarter-on-quarter. By June 2026, Vortexa/Kpler/Energy Aspects estimated forward
commercial-stock draws would run closer to **~1mb/d "in the coming months"** — roughly a third of
the Hormuz-related missing crude at that time — with the remaining two-thirds absorbed via
**reduced refinery runs and tightened fuel-export quotas** (a March 2026 informal directive
ordered top diesel/gasoline exporters to halt shipments; NDRC/MOFCOM tightened export-quota
oversight through Q2, favouring state-owned refiners). By September 2026, Kpler's preliminary
tracking put imports at **~7.2mb/d, roughly flat versus August** and still well below the
pre-war-adjacent February reading of 11.39mb/d — a persistent **~4.2mb/d shortfall** on that
comparison (calc), which is wider than the ~1mb/d "draw rate" estimate because part of the gap is
being absorbed by demand-side adjustment (lower refinery runs, export curbs) rather than by stock
draws alone.

**China's natural gas storage** buffer is comparatively larger relative to its role: underground
working gas storage capacity was an estimated **34bcm at end-2024**, against a stated
government plan of 55-60bcm by 2025 (one lower-confidence aggregator claims 67.45bcm already
reached in 2026, which conflicts with the more conservative plan figures and is flagged as
uncertain — use the 34-60bcm range). No China-specific LNG tank-inventory-in-days figure was
found; China's gas security leans more on this underground storage plus pipeline imports
(Russia/Central Asia) than on LNG tank days the way Japan's power-sector model does.

### 3. Drawdown trajectory (see `ws5_stock_timeseries.csv`)

Japan: total stockpile days fell from 254 (31 Dec 2025, pre-war) to 248 (31 Jan 2026, one month
into the war) to an estimated 215 (30 Apr 2026, post first release, pre second release — a
secondary-source figure). No official reading after that was located. Refinery utilization
recovered from crisis lows to 73.3% by 9 May 2026 as alternative (US/Caspian/Latin
America/non-sanctioned Russian) crude sourcing and stockpile releases took hold. No newer
utilization reading was found.

China: the clearest trajectory is the import series — 11.39mb/d (Feb, pre-war-adjacent) → 6.36mb/d
(May, trough) → 8.1mb/d (Q2 average, hard customs) → ~7.2mb/d (Aug/Sept, still-depressed plateau).
Implied monthly draw at the May peak was ~7.1mb/d (calc); by June the forward-looking estimate
from Vortexa/Kpler/Energy Aspects had eased to ~1.0mb/d, consistent with the partial import
recovery and refinery run cuts sharing more of the burden by mid-year.

### 4. Runway calculation (see `ws5_runway.csv` for full arithmetic)

**Methodology**: weeks of cover = usable stock ÷ **shortfall rate** (the gap between normal and
actual current supply), not usable stock ÷ total demand — because supply that continues to flow
normally (non-Hormuz routes) still meets most demand; reserves only need to fill the gap. Every
row in the CSV states its usable-stock derivation and shortfall assumption explicitly.

**Japan crude** — usable stock ≈ 247mn bbl (calc: national reserve treated as 100% usable at its
post-release level of ~111 days; private stock usable only down to the reduced 55-day legal floor,
≈26 days of headroom; joint reserve's remaining ~2 days; converted to barrels at an implied
consumption rate of 1.78mb/d, cross-validated two ways — 8.5mn kL/30 days and 80mn bbl/45 days
both imply the same rate).
- **Current/moderate** (May-2026-like conditions, most of the Hormuz gap already re-substituted,
  shortfall ≈200kb/d): **~176 weeks** of cover (floor ~2030) — comfortable, but this scenario is
  four months stale.
- **Full closure** (matching the post-9/11 reality — Hormuz transit ~3% of normal, all Saudi
  crude forced back through the strait): shortfall estimated **300-1,250kb/d**, giving
  **28-118 weeks** of cover depending on how much re-substitution occurs. This wide range is the
  single biggest data gap in this report for Japan — no September crude-import or refinery-run
  figure was located to narrow it.
- **Partial reopening**: shortfall ~100kb/d, cover effectively multi-year.
- **Naphtha is already at/near its floor** — crackers cutting output since March-May, independent
  of the crude-reserve runway.
- **LNG**: not a crude-style runway. The ~2-3 week utility tank-cover figure applies only to a
  full-stop scenario; the operative constraint through September 2026 has been **price** (JKM
  +118% y/y), not volume — JERA reports sufficient stocks through October.

**China crude** — usable stock ≈750mn bbl commercial (calc: 1,000mn bbl estimated commercial
inventory less an assumed 25% tank-bottoms/minimum-operating-inventory floor — an industry
rule-of-thumb, not China-specific-sourced) plus a further ~360mn bbl SPR treated as a backstop not
yet observed being tapped.
- **Current (Bloomberg/Vortexa-Kpler-Energy Aspects draw-rate estimate, ~1.0mb/d)**: **~107 weeks**
  (~2 years) of commercial cover.
- **Current (import-gap calc, 4.19mb/d)**: **~26 weeks** (~6 months) — the more conservative case,
  since it captures the full Feb-to-Sept shortfall rather than only the portion financed from
  stocks.
- **Full closure (May-2026 peak-stress analog, 7.14mb/d)**: **~15 weeks (commercial only)**,
  **~22 weeks including the SPR backstop**. This is the scenario most relevant if the post-9/11
  pipeline shutdown reproduces May-like severity.
- One source (dated ~June 2026, based on May data) called depletion toward operational minimums
  the analyst base case **for Q3 2026 — i.e., now**. This report cannot confirm that call with
  hard September data, and it sits in tension with China's partial easing of fuel-export curbs in
  July 2026 (see below).

### 5. What demand destruction looks like — and the legal machinery (not yet fully invoked)

**Japan**: the law that would govern formal rationing/allocation — the **Petroleum Supply and
Demand Optimization Act (石油需給適正化法, 1973)** — is distinct from the **Oil Stockpiling Act
(石油備蓄法)** that has actually been used for every release described above. This search pass
found **no evidence the Optimization Act has been invoked** in 2026; all measures to date (national
releases, the private-obligation cut, joint-reserve draws) have been stockpile-law actions, not
rationing-law allocation orders. The one sector already showing real demand destruction is
**petrochemical naphtha feedstock** — cracker run-rate cuts, announced March-May 2026, with the
Prime Minister publicly reassuring on 30 April that polyethylene supply (for food packaging) was
adequate "based on last year's demand" — i.e. residential/consumer-facing products were the ones
being actively defended, consistent with the brief's expectation that residential/essential uses
are politically protected while industrial feedstock is cut first.

**China**: no formal provincial power-rationing or fuel-allocation directive was found in this
search pass (flagged `NA` — a genuine gap, not a finding of "no such directive exists"). What *is*
documented is an **allocation hierarchy operating through export policy rather than domestic
rationing law**: an informal March 2026 directive ordered top diesel/gasoline exporters to halt
exports, prioritizing the domestic market; NDRC/MOFCOM tightened export-quota oversight through
Q2, with quotas going predominantly to state-owned refiners; and Beijing kept domestic pump-price
inflation to roughly 30% even as global product prices reportedly doubled — protecting consumers
via price control while pushing the adjustment onto refiner margins and export volumes. By July
2026 export curbs were **partially eased** (gasoline exports up 320% month-on-month, though still
down 55.3% year-on-year; diesel exports up 88% year-on-year amid a global diesel squeeze), which
reads as Beijing judging its domestic cushion adequate enough to resume some exports — a data
point in tension with the "depletion toward minimums by Q3" call above, and flagged as a
contradiction per evidence standard #6 rather than silently resolved.

### 6. IEA collective action

On **11 March 2026** IEA member countries agreed to release **400mn bbl** — the largest emergency
collective action in the agency's 52-year history (the sixth ever, after 1991, 2005, 2011, and
twice in 2022). Asia-Oceania members released immediately; Americas and Europe from end-March
(Europe's contribution weighted toward refined products, the Americas toward additional
production). The US alone committed **172mn bbl** from its SPR, the single largest national
share. At the time of the decision, IEA members collectively still held **>1.2bn bbl of public
emergency stocks plus a further ~600mn bbl of industry stocks under government obligation** — i.e.
the 400mn-bbl release drew down only a fraction of the total pool. **No running total of what
remains specifically from the March release, or of aggregate IEA member stocks as of September
2026, was found in this pass** — flagged `NA`. The release is explicitly a one-off buffer: it adds
supply to the market for a period but does not create ongoing production capacity, and every
barrel released is a barrel no longer available for a subsequent shock.

## Uncertainties and gaps

1. **Basis conflation risk (flagged per evidence standard #6)**: Japan's stockpile-day figures
   appear under at least two incompatible bases in public reporting — the shared brief's
   IEA net-import-cover basis (204 days, Jan 2025) and METI/ANRE's own domestic-consumption-law
   basis (254 days, Dec 2025). These are not the same year and not the same denominator; do not
   difference them to infer a drawdown.
2. **No Japan crude-import or refinery-utilization figure was located after 9 May 2026.** The
   "full closure" runway scenario for Japan (28-118 weeks) is consequently a wide, judgment-based
   range rather than a data-anchored point estimate — the single largest gap in this report.
3. **Japan LPG national reserve**: no current 2026 figure found at all; marked `NA` throughout.
   Flagged by the task brief as material — priority follow-up.
4. **Japan product-level (gasoline/gasoil/kerosene/jet/fuel-oil) stock levels**: not located for
   2026; PAJ's weekly publication appears to have changed format/availability around
   21 March 2026, and METI's own product statistics run ~2 months behind. Marked `NA`.
5. **China SPR figures are estimates by construction** — China publishes no official data. All
   China SPR/commercial numbers in this report carry `est` quality and should be treated as
   ranges, not precise values, per the task brief's explicit instruction.
6. **China's "depletion toward operational minimums, Q3 2026 base case" claim** (from a source
   dated ~June 2026, built on May 2026 data) could not be independently confirmed or refuted with
   September data in this pass, and is in tension with July 2026's partial fuel-export-curb easing
   (which suggests refiners felt comfortable enough to resume some exports). Both data points are
   reported; neither is resolved in favour of the other.
7. **China gas storage capacity for 2026** has two inconsistent estimates in the public domain —
   a conservative ~55-60bcm 2025 plan-based figure and a separate ~67.45bcm claim for 2026 from a
   lower-quality aggregator. Used the conservative range (34-60bcm) as primary; flagged the higher
   figure as unverified.
8. **China gas/LNG runway cannot be computed**: no total gas consumption rate or LNG-specific
   tank-days figure for China was found, so `weeks_of_cover` for the China natural-gas row in
   `ws5_runway.csv` is `NA` by necessity, not oversight.
9. **No provincial power-rationing or fuel-allocation directive was found for China**, and no
   confirmation that Japan's Petroleum Supply and Demand Optimization Act (as opposed to the
   separate Oil Stockpiling Act) has been formally invoked. Both are reported as "not found in
   this pass," which is weaker than "confirmed not invoked" — a further search pass with a larger
   budget could change this.
10. **Search budget**: this workstream's WebSearch allocation was shared across the session and
    was exhausted before several planned queries (Japan total oil demand mb/d from a primary
    source, China total oil demand mb/d, a September 2026 IEA Oil Market Report check, and
    further Japan LNG/LPG detail) could be run. Every derived ("calc") figure in the CSVs states
    its formula and inputs so gaps can be closed by re-running the missing queries rather than
    redoing the whole analysis.
11. **Unit conversions in `ws5_runway.csv`**: LNG and natural-gas rows express usable stock in a
    barrel-of-oil-equivalent (boe) conversion purely for schema/comparability purposes (LNG:
    51.6 MMBtu/tonne ÷ 5.8 MMBtu/bbl oil; gas: ~6.1 boe/1,000 m³). These are energy-content
    conversions, not physical substitutability — LNG and pipeline gas cannot be redirected into
    crude/product logistics.
